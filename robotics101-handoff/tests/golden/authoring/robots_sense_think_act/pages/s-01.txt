What is a robot? A robot is a machine that can sense the world around it, think about what it senses and then act.
A toaster is not a robot because it does not decide anything on its own; it only heats bread for a set time.
Robot vacuums sense dirt and obstacles and clean floors. In factories, robot arms assemble products piece by piece.
Space rovers explore distant places like Mars and can drive short distances on their own.
Most robots have five main parts: a structure, sensors, actuators, a power source and a controller.
The structure is the frame or body that holds all the other parts together.
Sensors gather information about the world, a little like our eyes and ears.
Actuators are the parts that move, such as motors that turn wheels or arms.
The power source, often a battery, supplies the energy the robot needs.
The controller is the robot's computer brain: it decides what the robot should do.
Robots repeat a loop: they sense, then think by comparing what they sense with their rules, then act.
